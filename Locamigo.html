Digital Product Management Bootcamp

LocAmigo

Helping newcomers find familiar ingredients, suitable local alternatives and the right stores when shopping in a new country.

Role Product Manager
Duration 3 months
Focus End-to-end product development
Output Product concept & prototype
01

The Challenge

Grocery shopping becomes surprisingly difficult after moving abroad.

Familiar ingredients have unfamiliar names, products look different and suitable substitutes are not always obvious.

“I had my phone in my hand the entire time and had to Google every product.”
Rafael · Brazil → Switzerland
“You have no idea what you will find or how much it will cost.”
Arzu · Turkey → England
“You basically just go shopping blindly.”
Anni · Germany → England
02

What We Learned

The problem isn`t translation. The real challenge was making confident choices in an unfamiliar supermarket.

Newcomers need more than a translated ingredient name. They need to know how the ingredient whether it is available or not, which possible substitutes will work in the same way, and where to find them in an unfamiliar supermarket environment.

Availability

Is the original ingredient commonly available locally? Under what name?

Alternatives

Are there subsitututes or do I have to visit a speciality store? Which local substitute will work in my recipe?

Stores

Where is the product most likely to be available?

Labels

What do unfamiliar names and packages actually mean?

The Market Opportunity

A growing problem, as the world gets more international.

05

Product Approach

Turning research into a focused product direction.

Research

Map the journey

Interviews and journey mapping identified the moments of greatest uncertainty before and during shopping.

Prioritisation

Focus the opportunity

Insights became a clear problem statement, value proposition and realistic MVP scope.

Delivery

Make it actionable

The concept was translated into a prototype, prioritised backlog and phased roadmap.

06

Outcome & Learning

From a broad idea to a focused product direction.

Hi-fi prototype

An interactive prototype demonstrating the core experience.

MVP backlog

A prioritised set of user stories for the first iteration.

Product roadmap

A Now–Next–Later roadmap for validation and expansion.

Business hypotheses

Initial assumptions around subscriptions and retail partnerships.

Key learning: Don’t fall in love with the solution. The real work was narrowing a broad idea into a meaningful first product.
