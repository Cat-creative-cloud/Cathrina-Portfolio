Market Intelligence Platform

How user research shifted an initial Tech Radar concept into a personalised market intelligence service built around daily relevance, faster consumption and continuous knowledge sharing.

Role Business Analyst
Focus Discovery, implementation and adoption
Context Global MedTech organisation
Impact Approx. 30% monthly user growth
01

The starting point

The initial idea was to build a Tech Radar.

The project began with the assumption that employees needed a structured overview of relevant technologies and market developments.

Before defining the solution, we interviewed potential users to understand how they currently stayed informed, which sources they relied on and where the process broke down.

02

Discovery

The interviews revealed a different need.

Stakeholders already had strong networks and access to valuable information. The challenge was not a lack of market intelligence.

Relevant articles were often forwarded through personal networks to colleagues who were known to be interested. Beyond those circles, however, the information rarely travelled.

The real need was not another static visualisation of technologies. Users needed a more efficient and continuous way to discover, consume and share relevant information.

03

The solution

A personalised stream of relevant market intelligence.

Instead of building a Tech Radar, we developed a central platform based on a dedicated database of relevant market and technology articles.

01

Curated article database

Relevant articles were collected in one central location and pre-filtered daily based on defined topics and areas of interest.

02

Faster consumption

Each article included a short abstract so users could understand its relevance without immediately reading the full source.

03

Customised newsletters

Users could subscribe to existing newsletters or configure their own based on the topics most relevant to their work.

Reflection

The strongest product decision was changing the original idea.

This project reinforced the importance of treating an initial concept as a hypothesis rather than a fixed solution. User interviews showed that the Tech Radar would not address the most relevant need. The greater value came from creating a continuous and personalised intelligence service.

It also taught me that adoption does not happen automatically after launch. For an internal product, communication, feedback and continued engagement are part of the product work itself.
